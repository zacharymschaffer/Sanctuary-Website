An invitation

Last December, a small circle of friends wrote a poem a day and shared it in a group chat.

The daily practice brought us closer—to our writing and to each other. This year, I’m opening the circle.

From December 1–31, write a poem each day and share it with a WhatsApp pod of five. Read each other. Offer a little encouragement. Some days, a poem might be four words; other days, it might surprise you.

The practice

  1. 01

    Write a poem.

    Any length, any form. A list, a fragment, four words, or something you’re not sure is a poem yet. It can be funny. It can be unfinished.

  2. 02

    Share it.

    Post it in your WhatsApp pod of five as text, a notebook photo, or a voice note. No need to explain it.

  3. 03

    Receive each other.

    Read your pod’s poems and respond with care. A line that landed, a brief note, or a simple reaction. No critique unless someone asks.

Take the commitment seriously. Let the poems be imperfect.

The month’s gentle shape

Four invitations.

Optional daily prompts offer a little inspiration for the year’s ending—with room for tenderness, nonsense, and whatever happened on the subway.

December 1–7

Gather

Collect a few things from the year: a scene, a sound, something you almost forgot. Start with what you notice and see where it takes you.

December 8–14

Bless

Offer your attention or an extravagant good wish to someone, something, anything. Tenderness and absurdity are equally welcome.

December 15–21

Release

Try a goodbye. Loosen a rule. Let something you’ve been holding find its way onto the page, gloriously unpolished.

December 22–31

Invite

Make room for something: a wish, a possibility, an unexpected guest. You don’t have to know where it’s going.

These are optional starting points, not assignments. Follow a prompt, bend it, or break it and write about something else entirely.

Before you ask.

What counts as a poem?

A poem can be a list, a fragment, four words, a voice note, or something you’re not sure is a poem yet. It can be funny. It can be unfinished. You don’t need to explain it.

What if I don’t know what to write?

Set a timer for five minutes. Start with something you can see, something someone said, or “I don’t know what to write, but…” Keep going without fixing it. When the timer ends, you can stop.

Do I have to use the prompts?

No. These are optional starting points, not assignments. Follow a prompt, bend it, or break it and write about something else entirely.

What if I miss a day?

Just write today. No apologies or catching up required.

Do I need to read everything before posting?

You don’t have to catch up on the chat before sharing today’s poem. Post first if that helps; return to reading when you can.

Do I need to attend gatherings or perform?

No. The daily writing and pod-sharing are the core commitment. Brooklyn gatherings are optional; come to whichever you can. Reading on January 8 is optional too.

What if something in my pod isn’t working for me?

If something in your pod feels uncomfortable or isn’t working for you, message me privately. You don’t have to sort it out in the group first.
